# glyphsift v0.2: Tamil design note

Status: **design only; not implemented.** No Tamil rule, table, weight or corpus exists in the repository, and
nothing here changes v0.1. Written in Sprint 4 (14 November 2026) after the team chose Tamil as the second script
and Bengali as the one after it (decision TD-18 in the status tracker; the owner can reverse it). The tests come
first when the work starts: the test table, not this note, is the specification.

## Why Tamil, and what it is meant to prove

The plan asks for a second script to test that the design is language-agnostic. Tamil tests that harder than
Bengali, which shares Devanagari's conjunct model, so most of the sixteen rules would carry over unchanged. Tamil
breaks three assumptions at once: its virama (the pulli) is a visible dot and, apart from a few Sanskrit-derived
ligatures such as க்ஷ, does not build conjuncts; some vowel signs are written on both sides of the consonant; and
there is no nukta. It also puts a Dravidian script beside Devanagari, which buyers outside the Hindi belt ask about
first, and one script fits a solo founder's grant scope.

## Scope

The Tamil block, `U+0B80..U+0BFF`: independent vowels, consonants including the five Grantha letters
(`U+0B9C` JA, `U+0BB6` SHA, `U+0BB7` SSA, `U+0BB8` SA, `U+0BB9` HA), the vowel signs, the pulli `U+0BCD`, the aytham
`U+0B83`, the anusvara `U+0B82`, the om sign, the digits and number signs (`U+0BE6..U+0BFA`). The Tamil Supplement block
(`U+11FC0..U+11FFF`, historical signs) is out of scope.

## What carries over from the sixteen Devanagari rules

The rules are about the order and combination of code points inside one grapheme cluster, so these carry over with a
Tamil code-point table behind them: a vowel sign with no base (`dependent_vowel_without_base`), a stray combining
mark, a vowel sign on an independent vowel, a pulli with no consonant before it (`virama_without_consonant`), a pulli
after a vowel sign (`virama_after_matra`), a script mixed into one cluster, an unexpected code point and a control
character. The joiner and tag rules stay limited to clusters whose base is in the script that uses them (the Sprint 3
rule); the open question for Tamil is below.

Three things need care, not a copy:

- **`double_matra` must know the two-part vowels.** `U+0BCA` O, `U+0BCB` OO and `U+0BCC` AU are single code points
  whose canonical decompositions are `U+0BC6 U+0BBE`, `U+0BC7 U+0BBE` and `U+0BC6 U+0BD7`. Text in either form is
  correct, and v0.1 does not normalise (a stated limitation), so the rule must accept exactly those three decomposed
  pairs, flag any other two vowel signs on one consonant, and flag a precomposed two-part vowel followed by another sign.
- **The aytham `U+0B83` is a letter** (general category Lo, despite its name "sign visarga"). It takes no vowel sign
  and no pulli, so the Devanagari visarga ordering rules do not apply to it.
- **The pulli does not join consonants in the segmenter.** Probed against the v0.1 build (`evidence/sprint-4/D3/tamil-probe.log`):
  under UAX #29 as implemented by `unicode-segmentation`, க்ஷ is two clusters (க் and ஷ), a consonant with a decomposed
  two-part vowel is one cluster, and a lone pulli or two pullis in a row are accepted without any report. So Tamil
  clusters are short, and the sequences for ksha (`U+0B95 U+0BCD U+0BB7`) and shri (`U+0BB6 U+0BCD U+0BB0 U+0BC0`) are valid
  across two clusters.

Not applicable: the three nukta rules (`double_nukta`, `nukta_after_matra`, `nukta_on_non_consonant`), reph and half-form
handling, and, as they stand, `sign_before_matra` and `visarga_before_anusvara`; `multiple_anusvara_like` is left
with a repeated anusvara only. Tamil has none of these constructions.

## What is new

- **Visual-order vowel signs.** `U+0BC6`, `U+0BC7` and `U+0BC8` are drawn to the left of the consonant but stored after
  it. A PDF text extractor or a legacy-font conversion that keeps the drawn order puts them first, at the start of a
  cluster. That is a `dependent_vowel_without_base` today and is expected to be the most common Tamil defect from
  extraction; the test table should have a case for each of the three and for the two-part vowels split around the
  consonant. Whether to name it as its own rule is decided when the cases exist.
- **Pulli placement.** A pulli right after a vowel sign, or a vowel sign right after a pulli, and two pullis in a row
  (accepted by v0.1 today) become cases. The Grantha letters with a pulli and a following consonant are valid.
- **The AU length mark** `U+0BD7` is valid only directly after `U+0BC6`; alone after a consonant it is not.
- **Joiners.** Whether `U+200C` after a pulli (used to stop the ksha ligature) is valid, and whether `U+200D` is flagged
  anywhere in a Tamil cluster, has to be settled from the Unicode Standard's Tamil chapter before any weight is set.

## Legacy encodings

Tamil text from older sources is often in 8-bit font encodings, not Unicode: TSCII, TAB and TAM, and font-specific
ones such as Bamini. Read as Latin letters they look like accented
Latin characters and punctuation, which v0.1 scores clean (the same blind spot as Kruti Dev, stated in the README).
The design is one document-level signal shared by all scripts, not a Tamil special case: runs of Latin-range
characters where a Latin word would not have them, with thresholds set from synthetic examples. It warns and does
not repair, since transcoding needs the exact font table. pagetriage already has a legacy-font check on a PDF's
text layer; this is the text-only backup for when that information is gone.

## Test table and corruption generator

- A new table of cases, `tamil.toml` beside `devanagari.toml`, written before any code: valid clusters (every
  consonant with every vowel sign, both forms of the three two-part vowels, pulli forms, the Grantha letters, ksha and
  shri), each defect above, and `known_gap_*` cases for what will not be caught (a well-formed wrong word, a
  visually plausible OCR confusion between two similar-looking letters).
- A synthetic Tamil clean corpus written for the benchmark (invented sentences, no copied text, a native speaker's
  review wanted, as for the Hindi corpus), with the same rule that every line validates clean.
- Generator changes (docs/corruption-generator.md): `virama_insert` and `virama_delete` become pulli families; `matra_double`,
  `matra_detach` and `matra_swap` take the Tamil vowel-sign set; `nukta_*` and `reph_misplace` do not apply; new families
  for visual-order vowel signs and for a two-part vowel with a missing or misplaced half. The sites, the counter-based
  draws and the integer-only probability stay as they are, so the benchmark criterion (the mixed score falls strictly
  as noise rises) applies to Tamil unchanged.

## The determinism promise does not change

No floating point, no hash collections, no clock or environment in the core crate; native, Python and WASM produce
byte-identical reports for valid Unicode text. A Tamil addition must pass `scripts/determinism.sh` with Tamil samples
added to `tests/determinism`. The report format needs a decision when the work starts: v0.2 reports mixed-script
documents, so the report may need a per-cluster or per-line script and a new report version (`glyphsift.report/0.3`)
rather than the single `script` field of 0.2.

## Bengali, later

Bengali follows after Tamil, when the Tamil work has shown which parts of the rule set were really
script-independent. It shares the Devanagari conjunct model, so the expectation is that most of it is a code-point
table plus the Bengali-specific parts (the khanda ta `U+09CE`, and the ya-phala and ra-phala conjunct forms), and that
is a smaller job than Tamil. Nothing about Bengali is designed here.

## Not decided here

The weights of any new rule, the corpus size, whether visual-order vowel signs get their own rule, the joiner rules and
the report version; each is settled by writing the test cases first. This is not a commitment to a date: the plan puts
the second script in months 4 to 6, backed by a grant that has not been submitted.
